# Video Music Phase (Stage 3 of the Short-Form Editing SOP)

Story Structure → Cutting & Pacing → **Music (this skill)** → Color Grading → Effects & Animation (5A → 5B) → Subtitles & On-Screen Text → Sound Design & Final Mix → Final QC.

Goal: music that supports the story and the feeling, never fights the voice, and lands its biggest moment on the payoff. Music shapes the pacing of the story, so it is decided early, together with the cut, not added at the end. The music serves the video, not the other way around.

Reply in the user's language. If they write in Taglish, answer in Taglish.

## When this stage runs

Pick the mode from the video, not by habit:

- **After-cut mode (default for speech-driven videos):** talking head, storytelling, tutorials, interviews. Stage 2 cuts to the words first; then this stage fits music to that cut.
- **Music-first mode (before Stage 2):** montages, food/product showcases, hype, highlights, or any video where the client already gave a track or the edit should follow the music. Pick and prepare the track here first, hand Stage 2 a beat map, then come back to confirm the lock.
- **Mixed videos:** pick the track first if any section is beat-driven; speech sections still follow the words.

Either way, the stage ends with a **cut + music lock**: the cut and the music fit each other before color, effects, text and sound design start. Later stages build on this lock instead of moving it.

## Scope

**This stage:** music mode, choosing the track, licensing check, beat map for Stage 2 (music-first), music edit (length, loops, endings), sync to story beats, rough music levels and a ducking plan under dialogue, and the cut + music lock.

**Not this stage:** cutting picture (Stage 2), SFX, dialogue cleanup, final ducking, final mix balance and loudness (Stage 7), export checks (Stage 8).

## Core rules

1. **Music needs a job:** set the mood, drive energy, carry a montage, mark a turn in the story, or deliver the payoff. If the video plays better without music in a section, leave it out there.
2. **Never last.** Music affects how the story is cut and paced, so it is decided in this stage, not after effects or sound.
3. **Story over beat.** Sync music to the story's beats (hook, turn, payoff, ending). Cut and music can go back and forth between Stages 2 and 3 until they lock, but don't cut essential content just to fit the track. Edit the music first; ask Stage 2 for a picture change only when it really helps the story.
4. **Dialogue is king.** Music sits under speech. Avoid songs with vocals under talking; use instrumentals or vocal-free sections there.
5. **Licensed music only.** Use music the user has the right to use for the purpose (personal vs. brand/client/ads). Flag risks plainly; don't promise a track is safe without knowing its license.
6. **One track, one beat map.** If Stage 2 cut to a BPM/track, keep that track or one with the same BPM and structure, or redo the beat map check.
7. **Be honest about limits.** Claude can't hear a track from its title alone. Describe recommendations as genre, tempo, mood and search keywords, and mark anything that needs a listen as "check by ear." If an audio file is uploaded, measure it (BPM, sections, loudness, peaks) in the workspace instead of guessing. Never reproduce song lyrics.

## Step 1 — Get the context (ask only what's missing)
- Story map / objective and the mood the viewer should feel
- Video type (speech-driven, montage, mixed) to choose the mode
- The cut or cut list from Stage 2 (after-cut mode), or the raw footage/story map (music-first mode)
- Platform and use: personal page, business/brand page, client work, paid ad (decides licensing)
- A track already picked, a reference video, or a vibe
- Editing app (CapCut, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, other)

## Step 2 — Music brief (when no track is picked yet)
Write a short brief:
- **Mood/energy:** warm, cozy, hype, cinematic, playful, emotional, premium, chill
- **Tempo range:** e.g. slow 70–90 BPM (emotional, ambient), mid 90–115 (lifestyle, food), fast 115–140+ (hype, sports, montages)
- **Structure needed:** a build into a drop near the payoff, a soft intro under the hook, a clean ending (not a fade-out unless it fits)
- **Vocals:** instrumental under talking; vocals OK in montage sections
- **Search keywords** for their library (CapCut/TikTok Commercial Music Library, YouTube Audio Library, Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Pixabay, etc.)
- **Licensing note** for their use case: brand/business accounts generally need commercial-cleared music; trending sounds may not be cleared for business use.

Give 2–3 direction options when the mood is open, each with a one-line reason tied to the story.

## Step 3 — Beat map for Stage 2 (music-first mode)
Before cutting, give Stage 2 a timing reference:
- BPM, beat and bar length (`60 / BPM` seconds per beat; 4 beats per bar)
- Section timecodes: intro, build, drop/chorus, breakdown, outro
- Where the hook, payoff/hero shot and ending should sit in the track
- Which part of the track to use if the full song is longer than the video

Then Stage 2 cuts to it, and this stage resumes at Step 4 to fine-tune and lock.

## Step 4 — Music edit and lock
- **Length:** cut the track to fit the video. Edit on bar or phrase boundaries (count in 4s or 8s), under a hit or in a transition, so the join is invisible. Crossfade a few frames at the join.
- **Start:** the music can start on the first frame, after the hook line, or on the first cut. A spoken hook usually wants a soft start or a short delay; a visual hook can start strong.
- **Payoff:** line up the drop, chorus or biggest accent with the story's payoff/hero moment. Shift the music start, cut a section out, or extend with a loop to make it land. Request a picture change from Stage 2 only if the music edit can't solve it and the story benefits.
- **Ending:** end on the track's real ending or a clean button/hit. Use a fade-out only when the mood calls for it. Don't let the music stop mid-phrase.
- **Story pauses:** where a reaction, punchline or emotional line needs space, dip or drop the music there. Stage 7 can refine these moments with sound design.
- **Lock check:** play the cut with the music at normal speed. If the pacing and music feel like one thing, lock it. Note any shot that should move by a few frames to hit an accent and settle it with Stage 2 now, not later.

## Step 5 — Rough levels and ducking plan
These are working levels so later stages can judge the video with music in place. The final balance and loudness happen in Stage 7.
- **Under speech:** music roughly 15–25 dB below the voice, low enough that every word is clear on phone speakers.
- **No speech (montage, B-roll):** music can come up near dialogue level and carry the moment.
- **Ducking plan:** mark where music ducks under speech and where it comes back up; use auto-ducking or keyframes (about 0.2–0.5 s duck time).
- **EQ note:** if music masks the voice even when quiet, note a midrange cut (around 1–4 kHz) under speech for Stage 7.

**App hints**
- **CapCut:** Beats (auto/manual markers), volume keyframes, Fade in/out, Audio "Ducking" on the music track.
- **Premiere Pro:** markers on beats, Remix tool for length, Essential Sound → Music → Ducking.
- **DaVinci Resolve:** beat markers on the timeline, Fairlight volume automation, Ducker / sidechain.

## Step 6 — Output: Music Plan
**1. Mode:** after-cut or music-first, and why.

**2. Track:** chosen track or the brief (mood, tempo, keywords, 2–3 options), plus the licensing status or risk.

**3. Beat map** (music-first or montage sections): BPM, section timecodes, where hook, payoff and ending sit.

**4. Music edit map** (timeline order):

| Time | Music event | Story beat | Action | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00.0 | Soft intro | Hook (spoken) | Start low, under voice | Duck −20 dB |
| 0:14.5 | Cut 8 bars | Build-up | Edit on bar, 4f crossfade | Keeps drop on payoff |
| 0:21.0 | Drop | Payoff / hero shot | Unduck, full level | No speech here |
| 0:24.8 | Dip to silence | Reaction pause | Cut music 1 beat early | Return on next bar |
| 0:30.0 | Ending hit | Closing | Clean button, no fade | — |

**5. Notes for later stages:** accents that transitions/graphics can hit (5A/5B), key music moments subtitles should respect (Stage 6), ducking plan, dips and EQ notes for the final mix (Stage 7).

**6. Flags:** "check by ear" items, licensing risks, picture changes agreed with Stage 2.

**Cut + music locked?** yes/no. **Ready for Stage 4 (color grading)?** yes/no and what still needs a decision.

## Step 7 — QC before moving on
- [ ] Mode chosen for the video (after-cut or music-first)
- [ ] Music fits the mood and objective of the story
- [ ] Music is cleared for this use (personal, business, client, ad)
- [ ] Every word is clear over the music on phone speakers at the working levels
- [ ] No vocals fighting the dialogue
- [ ] Edits on bars/phrases sound seamless
- [ ] Drop or biggest accent lands on the payoff
- [ ] Story pauses have room; ending resolves (no mid-phrase stop or lazy fade)
- [ ] Cut and music locked together; notes handed to 5A/5B, 6 and 7

If the music pulls attention away from the story, lower it, simplify it, or remove it.
