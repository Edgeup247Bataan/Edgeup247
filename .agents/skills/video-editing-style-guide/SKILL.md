# Video Editing Style Guide

A practical playbook for videos that earn attention, build curiosity, communicate emotion, and deliver a satisfying payoff. It is the **shared reference** behind the short-form SOP, not a separate workflow.

**Core principle: every edit should have a reason.** An edit should help the viewer notice something, understand the story, feel something, or stay oriented. Never recommend effects just to make a video look busy. When giving advice, tie each suggestion to the reason behind it.

## First: clarify the objective

Before advising, identify what the video is for. If the user hasn't said, infer it from context or ask one short question. Objectives drive every other choice:

- Inform, entertain, build trust, create desire, generate inquiries, or support a community.
- Platform and length (short-form vs. long-form), because pacing differs.
- Content type (see below), because each has its own editing language.

## The five psychology levers

Pick the lever the video most needs, then choose techniques that serve it.

| # | Lever | Goal | Edit moves |
|---|-------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Pattern interrupt | Attention | Unexpected opening frame, sudden close-up, abrupt sound cut, quick zoom, surprising action. Earn attention; don't mislead. |
| 2 | Curiosity gap | Retention | Teaser shot, partial reveal, reaction before the reveal, cutting just before the outcome. The video must eventually answer the question. |
| 3 | Sensory editing | Desire | Macro shots, crisp food sounds, sauce pours, textures, controlled slow motion, cuts timed to physical actions. |
| 4 | Emotional connection | Relatability | Candid reactions, natural dialogue, reaction inserts, intentional pauses, music that supports the feeling without overpowering it. |
| 5 | Reward and payoff | Completion | Progressive reveals, music payoff, final reaction, satisfying action, an ending that resolves the opening. |

## Hook-to-payoff structure

Default skeleton for a 30-second short-form video.

**Timing is a guide, not a rule.** Storytelling always comes before the stopwatch. If a beat needs more time than its window (for example, a build-up that runs past 15s because of the clip, dialogue, or a reaction that needs room), let it run. Never recommend cutting essential content, context, or emotional moments just to hit a time range. Only cut what doesn't move the story (repetition, dead time). When you give an edit plan, present the timings as approximate ("around 6-15s") and say plainly when a beat should stretch.

| Approx. time | Beat | Job |
|--------------|------|-----|
| 0-2s | Hook | Stop the scroll with the most interesting moment or a clear reason to pay attention. |
| 2-6s | Open loop | Introduce a question, conflict, or unfinished moment. |
| 6-15s | Build-up | Context, process, reactions, supporting detail. Cut repetition that doesn't move the story. |
| 15-24s | Payoff | The reveal, result, emotional moment, or satisfying shot. |
| 24-30s | Closing | A memorable image, brand cue, or natural next step. Don't force a CTA if it doesn't fit. |

For longer videos, keep the same logic (hook, open loop, build, payoff, close) and scale the beats. The same flexibility applies: the total length and each beat follow the story.

## Essential editing techniques (reference)

| Technique | How to edit | Purpose | Stage |
|-----------|-------------|---------|-------|
| J-cut | The next scene's audio begins before its video appears. | Creates anticipation and smoother transitions. | 5A |
| L-cut | The current scene's dialogue or audio continues over the next shot. | Keeps storytelling natural and connected. | 5A |
| Match cut | Two shots connect through similar movement, shape, framing, or composition. | Creates visual continuity or a satisfying association. | 5A |
| Speed ramp | The footage changes speed within a shot or sequence. | Emphasizes movement and adds energy when used selectively. | 5A/5B |
| Punch-in zoom | A quick crop or zoom emphasizes a word, reaction, or detail. | Directs attention; overuse can feel distracting. | 5A |
| Reaction insert | A reaction shot appears before or after an action or line. | Adds emotional context and can improve comedic timing. | 1–2 |
| Delayed reveal | The edit briefly withholds the key visual or answer. | Builds curiosity; avoid delaying so long that viewers feel tricked. | 1 |
| Sound bridge | Audio carries across a visual cut. | Creates flow and connects scenes. | 7 |
| Strategic silence | Music or ambience briefly drops out. | Creates contrast and gives a moment more impact. | 3, 7 |
| Visual reset | A change in framing, movement, scene, or visual information. | Renews attention and helps prevent a visually monotonous sequence. | 2/5A |

## Sound design psychology (reference)

**Music.** Sets the emotional direction: warm, tense, playful, cinematic, energetic, or reflective. Choose music that supports the story, and keep it under dialogue when needed.

**Sound effects (SFX).** Whooshes, clicks, impacts, transitions, and food sounds can emphasize actions. Use them only when they support the action or add useful emphasis.

**Dialogue and voice.** Make speech clear and remove unnecessary dead air, but preserve natural pauses that communicate personality or emotion. Avoid cutting breaths and pauses so tightly that people sound robotic.

**Silence and contrast.** A brief drop in music can strengthen a punchline, reveal, or important statement. Use contrast intentionally instead of layering sound continuously.

## Editing style by content type

**A. Food and product showcase:** macro shots of texture and ingredients; tight cuts during preparation and plating; realistic food sounds; controlled slow motion for key moments; a strong hero shot as the visual payoff. *Purpose:* sensory appeal, appetite stimulation, communicating product quality.

**B. Lifestyle and storytelling:** start with a relatable moment; retain authentic dialogue; use reaction shots and natural transitions; let emotional moments breathe; end with a meaningful visual or line. *Purpose:* relatability, identification, emotional connection.

**C. Behind-the-scenes:** show the process, not only the finished output; mix wide, detail and candid reaction shots; keep spontaneous moments if they add personality; use quick montages for repetitive tasks; include a final result or takeaway. *Purpose:* transparency, humanization, a sense of access.

**D. Sports, highlights and community:** open with a strong rally or reaction; sync cuts to the rhythm of the action; use score graphics only when relevant; prioritize actual gameplay and player reactions; end with a win, celebration or community moment. *Purpose:* anticipation, competition, social belonging, excitement.

## Big-picture edit plan

When the user wants a start-to-finish plan, outline it by SOP stage and point each part to its stage skill. Story comes before effects.

1. **Story Structure**: objective, hook, structure, story map.
2. **Cutting & Pacing**: remove repetition and dead time, keep pauses that help emotion, humor, clarity or realism.
3. **Music**: pick a licensed track that fits the story, edit it so the payoff lands, and lock the cut and music together. For montages or a given track, pick it before cutting and cut to the beat. Never leave music for the end.
4. **Color Grading**: fix exposure and white balance first, then one consistent look.
5. **Effects & Animation**: 5A visual variety through transitions, zooms and masks only where they help; 5B text and graphics readable on a phone, emphasizing only key phrases, inside safe areas, on-brand.
6. **Subtitles & On-Screen Text**: accurate, readable, inside safe zones.
7. **Sound Design & Final Mix**: clean dialogue, purposeful SFX, ambience, silence or sound bridges where helpful, then balance voice, music and SFX and set final loudness.
8. **Final QC**: watch once with sound and once muted; check format, aspect ratio, captions, audio peaks, spelling, brand details. After publishing, compare retention and engagement to the objective.

Only include the stages relevant to what the user asked. If they just want a hook, don't walk through all eight.

## Quick reference: match the edit to the goal

| Goal | Editing choices |
|------|-----------------|
| Earn attention | Strong first frame, unexpected action, clear opening line |
| Build curiosity | Teaser, open loop, partial reveal, unanswered but relevant question |
| Communicate quality | Clean composition, controlled movement, detailed close-ups, balanced sound |
| Create emotion | Authentic reactions, natural pauses, thoughtful music, room for dialogue |
| Make a process clear | Logical order, wide-to-detail coverage, labels when needed |
| Deliver satisfaction | Resolve the opening question, show the result, end on a strong image or reaction |
| Support conversion | Show the product or service clearly, communicate the value, make the next step easy when appropriate |

## Quick whole-video review

Use this for a fast critique or when the user asks "is this edit good?" (the Final QC stage does the full pre-export check). Walk through each item against the information they've given, and flag anything you can't judge without seeing the video.

- [ ] Is there a clear reason to stop scrolling in the first 1-2 seconds?
- [ ] Does the video have a clear story or progression?
- [ ] Are there unnecessary shots, pauses, or repeated information?
- [ ] Is there variation in shot size, movement, and pacing?
- [ ] Are dialogue, music, and sound effects clear and balanced?
- [ ] Do the cuts, transitions, and speed changes feel natural?
- [ ] Does the video deliver the payoff promised by the hook?
- [ ] Does the ending connect to the brand or content objective?

For each problem, name the stage that should fix it.

## Honesty about performance

Editing supports performance but cannot guarantee views or retention. Idea, audience fit, distribution, delivery, and platform context matter too. Keep these guardrails in your advice:

- **Match the hook to the actual payoff.** Never recommend a misleading opening the video doesn't fulfill. Delayed reveals shouldn't be so long that viewers feel tricked.
- **Fast isn't always better.** Slower pacing works when the story, performance, or visual detail earns attention.
- **Use data.** Retention graphs show where viewers leave; review what happens at those moments.
- **Test one major variable at a time** when practical: opening shot, hook wording, pacing, or length.
- **Track the metric that fits the objective**: watch time and completion for retention; saves and shares for usefulness or relatability; inquiries and bookings for conversion.
- **Use restraint with emphasis tools.** Punch-in zooms, SFX, and speed ramps lose impact when overused. Don't cut breaths and pauses so tight that people sound robotic.

Use psychology as a creative guide, then validate decisions with real audience feedback and performance data.

## Output style

Match the format to the request. A hook request gets a few concrete options. A big-picture plan gets a stage-by-stage outline with the reason for each choice. A critique names what works, what to change, why, and which stage fixes it. Keep suggestions specific (which shot, which sound, which moment) rather than generic.
